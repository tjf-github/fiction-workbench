# Huoren Write

当前版本：0.1

## 定位

把短剧剧本和网文小说改得更像真人写的。只处理表达层问题：去 AI 味、降机械感、增强自然度，同时保留原文事实、人设、人物关系和文本格式。

## 不可违反的边界

按以下顺序执行。下位规则不能违反上位规则。

1. 不新增剧情事实。不得发明人物、关系、案例、数据、日期、地点、背景、伏笔、反转、原因或后果。
2. 保留人设和声口。不能把所有人物都改成同一种随意口吻。
3. 保留原文功能。清理表达时，不能削弱原文已有的冲突、情绪、信息、伏笔、笑点或场景作用。
4. 保留格式。不能把剧本改成小说，也不能把小说改成剧本。
5. 宁可保留一点真人写作的粗糙和不完整，也不要改成过度光滑、对称、完整的标准答案。
6. 先拆 AI 模板，再加活人质感。
7. 遇到违规、低俗、侵权或平台红线风险时，只做表达层降敏和风险提示，不擅自新增替代剧情。

本 Skill 不负责设计故事节奏、钩子、爽点、集尾悬念或剧情结构。发现结构问题时，只能放到可选建议里，不得擅自写进正文，除非用户明确要求。

## 工作流程

1. 判断文本载体：短剧剧本、网文叙述、网文对话、旁白、OS、动作提示或混合文本。
2. 标记保护项：人名、身份、关系、时间线、地点、关键事实、人物声口和原有格式。
3. 选择执行力度：
   - 轻度：只清理明显 AI 痕迹和生硬表达。
   - 标准：默认档，清理大部分 AI 痕迹，同时尽量保留原文用词足迹。
   - 激进：AI 味很重时使用，句式重塑更明显，但仍不得新增事实。
4. 超过约 3000 个中文字符时，默认使用 bounded 模式：句内、段内表达可直接改，大结构调整只列建议。
5. 清理 AI 痕迹。文本存在模板句式、假精确、金句收尾、机械连接词、过度对称或过度完整论证时，读取 `references/ai-patterns.md`。
6. 增加活人感。清理后仍然显得太顺、太硬、太光滑、情绪太假时，读取 `references/human-texture.md`。
7. 校准人物声口。涉及对白、OS、具名人物或用户提供人设时，读取 `references/character-voice.md`。
8. 保留文本格式。处理剧本、剧本/小说混合文本、章节网文或带严格标签的文本前，读取 `references/format-preservation.md`。
9. 检查内容安全风险。文本涉及政治现实、色情低俗、违法犯罪、极端暴力、未成年人、现实人物机构、歧视或封建迷信等内容时，读取 `references/safety-and-banned-content.md`。
10. 自检并输出。最终交付前读取 `references/scoring-and-output.md`。
11. 只有在风格拿不准、用户要求示范或需要校准尺度时，才读取 `references/examples.md`。

## 输出格式

除非用户另有要求，按以下格式输出：

### 门检结论

- 文本类型:
- AI 味程度:
- 保护项:
- 执行力度:
- 处理模式:

### 打磨后正文

输出完整打磨后文本。尽量保留原有标签、场次、人物名、章节标记和段落布局。

### 改动说明

简要说明主要清理了哪些 AI 痕迹，以及做了哪些活人感调整。

### 残留与建议

列出刻意保留的内容、疑似假精确或事实风险，以及任何超出表达层改写范围、需要用户确认的大调整建议。

## 失败与边界处理

- 如果用户没有提供原文，要求用户补充文本。
- 如果用户要求新增剧情、设计钩子或调整故事结构，说明本 Skill 只做表达打磨，并询问是否切换为其他写作任务。
- 如果原文事实疑似有问题，不要靠猜测修正。正文中保留，放到 `残留与建议` 提醒。
- 如果原文涉及平台红线或违法违规风险，不要为了规避而编造新情节；只做表达降敏、删除细节化违规描写，并在 `残留与建议` 标注风险。
- 如果原文已经有明显真人质感，继续改会过度打磨，就只做最小修改并说明原因。

# AI 写作痕迹库

用这个文件识别和清理 AI 味。判断时看密度和违和感，不看单次出现。单个破折号、叹号、连接词或工整句子，不一定需要处理。

## 核心痕迹

1. 破折号、叹号滥用
   - AI 常用破折号制造解释感、转折感和强行强调。
   - AI 常用叹号制造虚假的情绪强度。
   - 保留必要标点，删掉装腔、表演式标点。

2. “不是……而是……”模板
   - 这种句式容易像议论文观点句或鸡汤总结。
   - 改成直接说人话，或拆成更自然的短句。

3. 强行凑三个词
   - 注意三连形容词、三连动词、三连抽象名词、三段并列。
   - 保留最有用的一个，或者换成动作和具体细节。

4. 句首高频连接词
   - 常见痕迹：此外、值得注意的是、本质上、事实上、实际上、换言之、从某种意义上说、总而言之、由此可见、不难看出。
   - 能删就删。确实需要过渡时，换成符合人物或叙述者口吻的自然衔接。

5. 段段金句、结尾升华
   - 删除段尾口号、人生道理、抽象总结和突然拔高主题。
   - 除非原文已经建立了这种人物风格，否则人物不要说 polished 的普世真理。

6. 节奏固定、匀速
   - AI 文常见句长相近、段落重量相近、情绪推进太平均。
   - 改成长短错落，允许少量不完整句，但不能改事实。

7. 全程没有犹豫
   - 真人会迟疑、躲闪、改口、重复，或者说不完整。
   - 只在符合人物和场景时加入，不要到处撒语气词。

8. 面面俱到，堵死反驳
   - AI 喜欢把所有角度都讲完，显得正确但不像人。
   - 删掉过度解释、过度自证、过度周全的部分。

9. 造假和假精确
   - 不新增案例、故事、数字、排名、年份、地点、人名、出处、权威来源或精确数量。
   - 原文里已有疑似假精确时，如果可能是剧情事实，正文保留，在最终说明里提示风险。

10. 过度对称和过度完整
   - AI 喜欢对仗、闭环、前后呼应、漂亮收束。
   - 放松结构。允许句子偏一点、断一点、没收满一点。

11. 万能安抚式回复
   - AI 常用“我理解你”“我就在这”“慢慢来”“我陪你”“如果你需要，随时找我”这类句子制造廉价陪伴感。
   - 创作文本里，除非人物本来就是咨询师、客服或极度温柔的人，否则这类话很容易让角色像机器人。
   - 改写时要按人物关系和当下情绪处理：亲近的人可以笨拙关心，敌对的人不会突然共情，寡言的人可能只做事不说安慰话。

12. 先夸再否定模板
   - 常见句式：你不是敏感，是太清醒；你没有疯，也没有乱；你现在不是情绪爆炸，只是太累了。
   - 这类句子像心理疏导话术，容易把人物台词改成情绪客服。
   - 改成具体态度或具体关系里的说法，不要套抽象安慰。

13. 复述接收模式
   - 常见句式：我听到了，我记住了；这次我只接，不反驳；你看见了，但你不一定要接。
   - 这种话在真实对话中很少自然出现，除非明确是治疗、咨询、调解场景。
   - 普通短剧和网文人物应改成更短、更笨、更有关系温度的话。

## 其他常见痕迹

- 空泛程度词：非常、十分、极其、格外、无比、前所未有。
- 脸谱化反应：嘴角勾起一抹冷笑、眼中闪过一丝精光、瞳孔地震、倒吸一口凉气、全场哗然。
- 情绪场里的抽象名词：能力、水平、认知、维度、态势、格局、底层逻辑。
- 被动和公文腔：被视为、得以、受到、予以、进行、做出、实现。
- 创作文本里的 Markdown 残留：项目符号、加粗口号、编号推理、不属于正文的解释性标题。
- 安抚客服腔：我理解你、我就在这、不急不乱、不躲不逃、你很好、我陪你一起走、如果你需要随时来找我、说一句不好听但是很真实的话。
- 流程托管腔：我们一步一步来、接下来我陪你、我逐步说清楚、不绕、我认真回答你、不敷衍。

## 网文小说专项痕迹

- 空洞华丽：词很贵，但信息很少，尤其容易出现在情绪戏和关系戏。
- 情绪标签化：直接写崩溃、震惊、绝望、心痛、治愈，却缺少当下动作或感知支撑。
- 主角心智过于完美：人物瞬间理解一切、反应总是最优、说话像冷静旁白。
- 伪装成对白的说明书：人物解释双方都知道的规则、背景、动机或关系。
- 复用氛围词：月光皎洁、夜色如墨、空气凝固、时间仿佛静止、命运齿轮、心脏漏跳一拍。
- 伪文学收尾：每段都落在一个漂亮比喻、道理或情绪总结上。

## 短剧专项痕迹

- 台词替观众总结剧情：人物把隐含关系、冲突或背景说得过于整齐。
- 工具人反应：配角只负责喊、惊、问、夸，而且节奏一样。
- 标签式动作提示：动作行解释情绪，而不是给演员可表演的行为。
- 万能狠话：你会后悔的、我不会放过你、你根本不知道我是谁、你这是在玩火。
- OS 过度直白：内心独白直接说出完整道理或动机，缺少真实人的混乱感。
- 表演式标点：对峙台词每句都用“！”或“？！”收尾，导致情绪一样吵。
- 温柔机器人台词：角色在冲突中突然开始“接住情绪”“稳定陪伴”“确认感受”，但这不符合人物关系或场景压力。

## 改写策略

- 先删，再换。
- 一个准确的动词，优先于三个虚的形容词。
- 只有原文已经支撑该情绪时，才把抽象情绪换成动作或身体反应。
- 把议论文式过渡改成自然的局部流动。
- 保留有用的粗糙感，不要改得太雅。
- 对白里删掉双方都知道的信息，除非格式确实需要交代。
- 叙述里遇到伪文学收束，可以换成更落地的观察，也可以直接提前停住。
- 遇到万能安抚句，先判断人物关系：能沉默就沉默，能做事就做事，能说狠话就别安慰。
- 把“正确的心理话术”改成“这个人会说的笨话、短话、别扭话或回避话”。

# 人物声口

处理对白、OS、具名人物，或用户提供人物设定时，使用这个文件。

## 声口锚点

改对白前，先从原文推断或向用户收集：

- 人物名:
- 身份和角色:
- 年龄段:
- 教育程度、阶层、文化水平:
- 和其他说话人的关系:
- 当前情绪:
- 说话习惯:
- 禁用表达:
- 代表台词:
- 遇到压力时的反应方式:

如果用户只给原文，就只能谨慎推断，不要发明背景。

## 声口区分

- 年轻人/学生：可以直接、松一点、略重复、情绪外露。避免过度成熟总结。
- 底层/普通打工人：多用大白话和具体关切。除非原文已有，不要说“认知维度”“底层逻辑”。
- 长辈/权威人物：常见短句、慢一点、带判断或说教。不要改得太网感。
- 高位角色：可以克制、短促、绕一点。不要塞太多语气词。
- 寡言角色：减少字数，让压力留在停顿、动作或省略里。
- 反派/对立角色：避免万能狠话。威胁、嘲讽、轻蔑要贴合身份和关系。
- 叙述者：保留原本叙述距离，不要随便切换视角。
- 温柔/照顾型角色：可以安慰，但不要像心理咨询模板。真正亲近的人常常说得不完整，甚至有点笨。
- 客服/咨询师型角色：只有当原文明确是这类身份时，才允许较完整的接纳、复述和安抚话术。

## 对白清理

- 打破完美问答节奏。
- 允许打断、躲避、延迟回答或只答一半。
- 删除听起来像给观众解释剧情的台词，除非原格式确实需要交代。
- 保留人物专属的粗糙感，即使句子不是最优雅。
- 删掉“双方都知道”的说明式对白。如果两个人都知道这个事实，就把台词改成态度、压力、威胁、羞耻或回避。
- 尽量保留潜台词。人经常绕着真正想说的事说。
- 同一段冲突里，让不同人物的反应方式不同：有人躲，有人逼问，有人讥讽，有人沉默。
- OS 要比口头对白更私密，不要像公开演讲。
- 删除万能安抚模板。比如“我理解你”“我在这里”“如果你需要随时找我”，要改成符合关系的具体说法或具体行动。
- 不要让人物先给对方贴心理标签，再给出标准安慰。人物可以误解、急躁、嘴硬、不会安慰。

## 声口压力测试

接受改写后的台词前，先问：

- 这个人会对这个听众这么说吗？
- 他会说得这么清楚，还是会藏一部分？
- 这句话是说给对方听的，还是说给观众听的？
- 这句话像这个人物，还是像一个永远正确的心理咨询回复？
- 这句安慰是否太会说、太稳、太懂了？
- 改写后有没有让人物比原文更聪明、更体面、更会说话？
- 改写有没有新增原文没有的态度、记忆或信息？

## 禁止动作

- 不要让所有人物都变成同一种“活人感”口吻。
- 不要为了让台词鲜活而新增人物过往。
- 不要在没有原文支持时改变人物关系温度。
- 不要把人物改得比原文更聪明、更善良、更刻薄或更能说会道。
- 不要使用方言、黑话、口头禅，除非原文已有或用户提供。
- 不要把普通人物改成“情绪价值提供者”。
- 不要把冲突台词改成互相理解、互相接住的标准答案。

# 活人感打磨规则

清理 AI 痕迹之后，如果文本仍然太顺、太正确、太光滑、太像生成答案，就用这个文件补活人感。

## 基本原则

- 文本要像那个场景里的人写出来、说出来，而不是像模型在总结这个场景。
- 保留一点粗糙。不要把每句话都磨成文学化、聪明、对称的成品句。
- 只能在原意内部增加活人感，不能新增剧情事实。

## 具体方法

1. 打散句长
   - 混用短句、中句和稍长句。
   - 情绪强的地方可以突然变短。
   - 避免每段都差不多重。

2. 使用自然衔接
   - 可用：说白了、其实、话说回来、可问题是、你别说、怎么讲、也不是不行。
   - 少用，只在叙述者或人物真的会这么说时使用。

3. 允许小瑕疵
   - 可以保留少量重复、迟疑、改口、半句话。
   - 不要把每句都改成标准答案。

4. 用局部感受替代大道理
   - 优先写人物看见、听见、感觉到、注意到的东西。
   - 例如把“他感到无比愤怒”改成原文支撑的动作：“他手指攥紧了点”。

5. 降低过度解释
   - 真人不一定把所有动机讲清楚。
   - 保留理解所需的信息，删掉防御式、全角度式解释。

6. 保持直白
   - 能用普通话就不用术语。
   - 场景允许时，用“赚钱”替代“实现变现”，用“说清楚”替代“完成沟通闭环”。

7. 让念头没想完
   - 人在压力下常常不是完整作文，而是碎片念头。
   - OS 和内心戏里，可以保留怀疑、躲闪、自相矛盾或半句话。

8. 短剧动作提示要能演
   - 动作提示优先写演员能做出来的行为，不写情绪标签。
   - 例如把“她十分悲伤”改成原文支撑的可表演行为：“她把话咽回去，低头捏着袖口”。

9. 普通话就让它普通
   - 不是每句话都要有巧思。
   - 人物只是想让对方停下时，可以直接说：“别说了”“你先出去”“我不想听”。

10. 给情绪留空
   - 不要刚展示完情绪，马上解释这是什么情绪。
   - 沉默、短句、没说完的话，都可以承担一部分情绪。

11. 安慰要像人，不要像模板
   - 真人安慰常常笨、短、绕，甚至说错话。
   - 不要默认使用“我理解你”“我陪你”“你很好”“慢慢来”这类万能句。
   - 可以用具体动作替代安慰台词：递水、站近一点、岔开话题、骂一句、沉默陪着。

## 防止过度修正

- 不要到处加语气词。
- 不要让严肃、高位、寡言角色突然变碎嘴。
- 不要把网络流行语硬塞进古风、正式或成熟语境。
- 除非原文已有这种叙述姿态，否则不要把旁白改成随口吐槽。
- 不要为了“自然”削弱原文里的锋利台词。
- 不要把每个抽象句都替换成新感官细节，那可能会新增事实。
- 不要刻意把文本改笨。目标是像人，不是像低质量。
- 不要让每个角色都拥有稳定、温柔、正确的情绪处理能力。

</skill_resource>

# Line Editing

## Overview

Own the prose-quality passes that come after structure is settled: the
line edit (sentence clarity, rhythm, precision, voice), character-voice
differentiation, the copyedit against `style-sheet.md`, a read-aloud pass,
and a proof pass on a built copy. Every change is proposed with a
before/after and a one-line rationale so the author can accept or reject
it. The author's voice is the standard, not the agent's taste. The
`better-writing` skill, when installed, is an optional complement for
anti-generic checks; this skill does not depend on it.

## Prerequisites

A story project with `story.md` and drafted chapters (status `draft` or
later). Read `style-sheet.md` if present; if it is missing or thin, build
it first with the `voice-style` skill, because the copyedit checks against
it.

## Language

Read `language` in `story.md` (a BCP 47 tag such as `en-GB`, `fr`, or
`ja`; a missing field means `en`). Edit in that language and to its
grammar and conventions, and write edit notes in the language the user
works in with you, quoting the prose exactly as written. The checklists'
word-level advice (filter words, -ly adverbs, *said*, dialogue
punctuation) is English: for another language, apply its aim, not its
word lists, and take dialogue and punctuation from the style sheet and
`references/language-conventions.md`. When `story prose` or
`story voices` reports a check skipped for the book's language, or a
check plainly does not fit it, do that pass by reading.

## When to Use

- A chapter's structure is settled and the prose needs polish
- Dialogue sounds the same from every speaker
- Preparing a manuscript for beta readers, an editor, or submission
- The `line`, `copyedit`, or `proof` revision pass is next in `story passes`
- NOT for structural, plot, or continuity revision (use `revision-continuity`; line-edit only after those passes, or the polish is wasted)
- NOT for setting house style, the voice description, or each character's recorded voice (use `voice-style`; this skill applies them)
- NOT for scene-level craft such as deep POV, subtext, or exposition strategy (use `scene-craft`)
- NOT for acting on external reader notes (use `feedback-triage`)
- NOT for writing or scanning verse (use `verse-craft`); this skill only
  flags rhymes that slipped into prose by accident

## Workflow

### 1. Scope and permission

1. Ask which chapters and which pass: line edit, voice differentiation,
   copyedit, read-aloud, or proof. Default order is the one below.
2. Ask how heavy the edit should be: **light** (errors and clear
   improvements only), **medium** (tighten and clarify), or **heavy**
   (restructure sentences and paragraphs). Default to light.
3. Never rewrite a passage wholesale without explicit permission. Propose
   edits; apply only what the author accepts, or apply all on explicit
   instruction.
4. Snapshot before a multi-chapter pass (see Draft Snapshots in the
   `revision-continuity` skill), and mark the pass in progress. If
   `story.md` has no `revision-passes` yet, `story passes . --init` writes
   the default ladder first; `story passes .` shows where the book is.

```shell
story passes . --start line
```

### 2. Line edit

1. Read `story.md` (POV, tense, Tone & Style), the style sheet's Voice
   section, and the chapter. Run `story prose .` for the chapter's counts.
   If the style sheet has no `samples`, suggest the author list an
   earlier book or chapter files they are happy with (see the `voice-style`
   skill), so the counts are measured against their own voice. A
   `prose-baseline-*` warning shows where a chapter drifts from it: use
   it to find passages to reread, and keep any drift the author meant.
2. Work paragraph by paragraph with `references/line-edit-checklist.md`:
   clarity, precision, economy, rhythm, POV distance, and voice.
3. Present edits in the format in `references/edit-note-format.md`:
   location, before, after, and one line of rationale. Group by
   paragraph; lead with the highest-impact changes; cap a batch at about
   20 so the author can review them.
4. Leave passages that are unusual but deliberate. When unsure whether a
   quirk is voice or error, ask.
5. To check a rewritten passage before putting it in the chapter, pipe it
   in: `story prose - < rewrite.md` lints it against the style sheet, and
   `story voices - < rewrite.md` checks its dialogue against the
   characters' `voice-avoid` lists.

### 3. Differentiate character voices

```shell
story voices .
```

The report fingerprints each character's attributed dialogue (sentence
length, contractions, questions, exclamations, signature words) and warns
when two voices are near-identical, when a character says a
`voice-avoid` word, and when a `voice-words` entry never appears.

1. For near-identical pairs, read both characters' Voice & Speech
   Patterns sections and propose line-level changes that follow them:
   vocabulary, sentence length, what each avoids saying, how each deflects.
   See the voice section of `references/line-edit-checklist.md`.
2. If a character file has no voice notes, propose `voice-words` and
   `voice-avoid` lists drawn from their best existing lines, and ask
   before adding them to the character file.
3. A line counts only when the narration names its speaker by a speech
   verb, or names one character in the paragraph. Pronoun tags
   (`she said`) and untagged lines are invisible to the report, so the
   POV character in close third is often under-counted; read those lines
   by hand, or name the tags in a sample chapter and rerun.
   `story voices --help` describes the command and, when
   `story-maintenance` is installed, the voices section of
   [`../story-maintenance/references/continuity-checks.md`](../story-maintenance/references/continuity-checks.md#voices)
   gives the full attribution rules and thresholds.

### 4. Copyedit

1. Mark the pass: `story passes . --start copyedit`.
2. Run `story prose .` and fix every avoided spelling. Then work through
   `references/copyedit-checklist.md` against `style-sheet.md`: grammar,
   punctuation, dialogue punctuation, capitalisation, hyphenation,
   numbers, and consistency of names and terms (check the glossary). For
   a book not in English, take dialogue and punctuation conventions from
   `references/language-conventions.md`; if the style sheet has not
   recorded them, settle them with the author first.
3. Record every new decision in `style-sheet.md` in the same change, so
   the next chapter follows it.
4. Tell the user plainly: this is a consistency and correctness pass, not
   a substitute for a professional copyeditor on a book going to print.

### 5. Read-aloud pass

Follow `references/read-aloud-guide.md`. Build the narration script:

```shell
story build . --format narration
```

Offer to play chapters with the system's text-to-speech if one is
installed (`say` on macOS; `espeak-ng` or `spd-say` on Linux). Check with
`command -v`; ask before installing anything. Choose a voice for the
book's `language` (`say -v '?'` and `espeak-ng --voices` list them); an
English voice reading another language is no test of the prose. Listen for stumbles,
unintended rhymes, tongue-twisters, and runs of same-length sentences;
record them as edit notes.

### 6. Proof pass

1. Mark the pass: `story passes . --start proof`.
2. Build the copy the reader will see:

```shell
story build . --format html
story build . --format print --trim 6x9
```

3. Proof against `references/copyedit-checklist.md`'s proof section:
   typos introduced by editing, doubled or missing words, broken scene
   breaks, chapter headings, matter pages, widows and orphans in the print
   copy. Cite locations by the HTML copy's paragraph anchors (`ch03-p12`).
4. To proof the print copy as a PDF, add `--pdf` to the print build:

   ```shell
   story build . --format print --trim 6x9 --pdf
   ```

   It finds and runs a paged-media engine the user has installed (Prince,
   WeasyPrint, `pagedjs-cli`, or Chrome as a fallback) and writes
   `dist/<story-id>.pdf`. Name an engine with `--pdf-engine <name|path>`.
   With none installed it stops (exit 4) and lists what to install; the
   CLI bundles none. Ask before installing anything.

### 7. Close the pass

1. Summarise what changed, what was kept on purpose, and any style-sheet
   or character-file updates.
2. Update chapter `status` only when the author agrees (`draft` to
   `revised`).
3. Mark the pass done, for example `story passes . --done line`, and run
   CLI Maintenance.

## Conventions

- The author's voice wins. Edit toward what the prose is already trying
  to do, not toward a generic "good prose" standard.
- One rationale line per change, naming the effect (*clearer subject*,
  *cuts a filter word*, *restores past tense*), never "sounds better".
- Edits are made directly in the chapter markdown, below `## Chapter
  Text`; never through a script that rewrites prose in bulk.
- Style decisions go in `style-sheet.md`; voice decisions about a
  character go in the character file (`voice-words`, `voice-avoid`, Voice
  & Speech Patterns), which stays canon.
- A line edit does not change events, facts, or who knows what. If an
  edit would, stop and hand it to `revision-continuity`.

## CLI Maintenance

Use the Story CLI when it is available. If `story` is not installed, use the
bundled fallback `node ../story-maintenance/scripts/story.js` with the same
arguments. Use `node <checkout>/bin/story.js` instead only when the user
names a Story Skills repository checkout or you are working in one. Write
the script as an absolute path (resolve the fallback relative to this skill
folder) and run it from the folder you would run `story` from, so `.` and
other relative paths keep their meaning. Use Node, not Bun or a package
script: Bun would load that folder's `bunfig.toml` (which can run code) and
`.env`, and a package script runs from the checkout's root. If no CLI is
available, apply the checklists by reading and keep word counts current by
hand.

After editing chapters, character voice fields, or the style sheet:

```shell
story reindex .
story wordcount . --write
story check .
story prose .
story voices .
```

<skill_resource name="line-editing-checklist.md">
# Line Edit Checklist

Work one paragraph at a time. For each check, the question comes first;
the fix is a suggestion, and the author's voice overrides it. Match the
edit weight the author chose (light, medium, heavy).

## Clarity

- **Who is doing what?** Each sentence's subject and verb should be easy
  to find. Buried subjects (*It was the sound of the bell that woke her*)
  become direct (*The bell woke her*) unless the delay is the point.
- **Pronoun reference.** Every *he*, *she*, *they*, *it* points to one
  person or thing. Two same-gender characters in a paragraph is where this
  breaks; use a name.
- **Sequence.** Actions appear in the order they happen, and cause before
  effect: *She flinched when the door slammed* reads backwards at speed;
  *The door slammed. She flinched.* reads in order.
- **Dangling modifiers.** *Running for the ferry, the rain soaked her* has
  the rain running.

## Precision

- **Concrete over general.** *A bird* to *a gull*; *food* to *cold
  chips*. One precise noun beats an adjective stack.
- **Strong verbs.** Adverb plus weak verb (*walked slowly*) to one verb
  (*trudged*), when a better verb exists and fits the voice.
- **Right word.** Near-miss words (*enormity* for size, *disinterested*
  for uninterested) and words from the wrong era or world.
- **Figurative language.** Each metaphor comes from the POV character's
  world, fits the tone, and does not mix with the one beside it. Cut
  figures that explain what the plain sentence already showed.

## Economy

- **Redundancy.** *Nodded her head*, *shrugged his shoulders*, *stood up*,
  *sat down*, *thought to herself*.
- **Throat-clearing.** *She began to*, *started to*, *seemed to*,
  *managed to*, unless the incompleteness matters.
- **Stacked hedges.** *Almost*, *just*, *a little*, *somewhat*, *kind of*.
  Keep them in dialogue where they are characterisation.
- **Stage directions.** Movement the reader does not need (crossing the
  room, turning to face) between two lines that matter.
- **Double telling.** An emotion named after the action already showed
  it. Cut the naming.

Do not cut for its own sake. A light edit trims only repetition and
clutter; lush or maximal voices keep their length.

## Rhythm

- **Sentence length follows intent.** Short for impact, long for flow or
  overwhelm. `story prose .` flags uniform spread; fix by intent, not by
  alternating lengths mechanically.
- **Paragraph endings.** The last words of a paragraph carry weight. End
  on the image or the turn, not on a trailing clause.
- **Echoes.** Repeated words within a few lines (the prose report lists
  them). Keep deliberate repetition.
- **Sound.** Accidental rhyme, alliteration, and tongue-twisters; the
  read-aloud pass catches these best.

## POV and distance

- **Filter words.** *She saw*, *heard*, *felt*, *noticed* put a pane of
  glass between reader and scene. Remove them where the perceiving is not
  the point. See `../../voice-style/references/prose-checks.md`. The
  word list is English; in another language, look for the same effect
  (verbs of perceiving that announce instead of show) by reading.
- **Head-hopping.** Only the POV character's thoughts and feelings are
  known directly; others' show through behaviour.
- **Knowledge slips.** The POV character names things they could not
  know or would not notice.
- **Tense and person.** Consistent with `story.md`'s `tense` and `pov`.

## Voice

- **Consistency with the Voice section of `style-sheet.md`.** Distance,
  register, rhythm, and what the prose never does.
- **Genericness.** Stock phrases (*a beat of silence*, *let out a breath
  she didn't know she was holding*) replaced with the specific thing this
  character would notice. Add real tics to `watch-words`.
- **Don't flatten.** Fragments, dialect, run-ons, and odd syntax that are
  part of the voice stay. When in doubt, ask.

## Character-voice differentiation

When `story voices .` reports two voices near-identical, or dialogue
reads interchangeably, use these levers, one or two per character, drawn
from their character file:

| Lever | Examples |
|-------|----------|
| Sentence length and completeness | Clipped fragments vs full, subordinate-heavy sentences |
| Contractions and register | *I will not* vs *I won't* vs *'m not* |
| Vocabulary domain | Trade, class, region, era, education; what their metaphors come from |
| Questions vs statements | Deflects with questions; never asks one |
| Signature words (`voice-words`) | A pet phrase, oath, or verbal habit used sparingly |
| Forbidden words (`voice-avoid`) | Never swears; never says *sorry*; never uses a name |
| What they avoid talking about | Changes the subject, jokes, goes silent |
| Directness | Says what they mean vs talks around it |

Test: cover the tags on a page of dialogue. The author should be able to
tell who is speaking from the line alone for the main cast. After edits,
rerun `story voices .` to confirm the fingerprints moved apart.

</skill_resource>

# Read-Aloud Guide

The ear catches what the eye skips: stumbles, accidental rhymes, a
sentence too long to say in one breath, three paragraphs with the same
rhythm, dialogue no one would speak. A read-aloud pass is the cheapest
line edit there is.

## Build the narration script

```shell
story build . --format narration
```

The script is markdown with a pronunciation guide (from every
`pronunciation` field on characters, locations, factions, artifacts, and
glossary terms), each chapter with an estimated runtime at 155 words per
minute, scene breaks marked `[pause]`, and a total runtime. Missing
pronunciations for invented names are a finding: add a `pronunciation`
scalar (for example `SEER-sha`) to the entity file.

## Listening options

1. **The author reads aloud.** Best, because the author hears their own
   intended rhythm. Suggest it for key chapters (opening, midpoint,
   climax, ending).
2. **System text-to-speech.** Flat but honest: a synthetic voice does not
   smooth over awkward syntax. Check what is installed before offering:

   ```shell
   command -v say espeak-ng spd-say
   ```

   - macOS: `say -f chapter.txt` or `say -o chapter.aiff -f chapter.txt`
   - Linux: `espeak-ng -f chapter.txt` or `spd-say "$(cat chapter.txt)"`
     (short passages)

   Extract only the chapter prose into a scratch text file outside the
   story project, and delete it afterwards. **Ask before installing any
   package.** If nothing is installed, the author's own reading or their
   device's built-in screen reader works.
3. **The agent reads for sound.** Without audio, read each sentence for
   breath length, stress pattern, and repeated sounds. Weaker than
   listening; say so.

## What to listen for

| Symptom | Likely cause | Fix |
|---------|--------------|-----|
| Stumble or re-read | Garden-path syntax, ambiguous modifier | Reorder so the subject and verb come early |
| Run out of breath | Sentence too long for its content | Split at the turn, or make the length earn itself |
| Sing-song | Same length and stress, sentence after sentence | Vary by intent: a fragment, a long cumulative line |
| Unintended rhyme or jingle | Sound echoes (*the light was bright tonight*) | Swap one word |
| Tongue-twister | Clustered sibilants or plosives | Rephrase, unless the tongue-twist is the effect |
| Dialogue sounds written | Complete, grammatical, speech with no interruption | Contractions, fragments, interruption, subtext |
| Same voice for everyone | Undifferentiated dialogue | See the differentiation levers in `line-edit-checklist.md`; run `story voices .` |
| Name mispronounced | Invented name with unclear stress | Add `pronunciation`; consider a simpler spelling |
| Drag | Scene summary or exposition between beats | Mark for `scene-craft` or `revision-continuity` |

## Recording findings

Record each finding as an edit note (see `edit-note-format.md`) with the
chapter and paragraph, the problem heard, and a proposed fix. Group by
chapter. The narration runtimes are also useful for pacing: a chapter far
longer to hear than its neighbours is worth checking with `story pacing .`.

## Picture books and chapter books

For `picture-book` and `chapter-book` forms, read-aloud is not optional:
the text will be read aloud by an adult to a child. Listen for page-turn
rhythm, refrains, and words a young listener cannot parse.
